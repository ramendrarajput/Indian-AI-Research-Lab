# Purpose

The **Archive Department** is responsible for preserving the historical memory of Project BRAHMA.

Knowledge evolves over time.

Ideas improve.

Theories change.

Engineering advances.

Documents are rewritten.

But history should never be erased.

Archive exists to preserve that history.

---

# Mission

The mission of the Archive Department is to maintain a permanent, traceable, and verifiable history of the evolution of Project BRAHMA.

Every archived document represents a step in the journey of understanding.

Nothing important is ever truly deleted.

---

# Philosophy

Project BRAHMA believes that scientific progress is not a straight line.

Every successful theory was once an unfinished idea.

Every refined model was once an imperfect draft.

Every discovery carries the memory of earlier attempts.

Therefore,

**The history of knowledge is itself knowledge.**

# What Belongs in the Archive?

The Archive Department stores historical records that should be preserved but are no longer part of the active development process.

Examples include

- Previous versions of documents
- Deprecated hypotheses
- Historical research notes
- Old architecture diagrams
- Legacy software
- Experimental prototypes
- Earlier publications
- Previous presentations
- Obsolete datasets
- Historical releases

---

# What Does NOT Belong Here?

Archive is **not** a trash folder.

Temporary files should never be archived.

Broken experiments that provide no historical or scientific value should not be preserved.

Only historically meaningful material belongs here.

---

# Archive Principles

## Preserve History

Do not erase scientific history.

Preserve it.

---

## Never Rewrite the Past

Historical documents should remain unchanged.

Corrections belong in newer versions, not by modifying historical records.

---

## Traceability

Every archived item should clearly indicate

- Why it was archived
- When it was archived
- Which newer version replaced it

---

## Scientific Transparency

The evolution of ideas should remain visible.

Future researchers should be able to understand

- how an idea originated,
- how it changed,
- and why it evolved.

---

## Long-Term Preservation

Archive is designed for decades, not days.

Documents stored here should remain readable and meaningful for future generations.
