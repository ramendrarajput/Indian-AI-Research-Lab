The Book of Questions is the intellectual heart of Project BRAHMA.

# Preface

Every civilization is remembered not only for the answers it discovered,

but for the questions it dared to ask.

Questions created mathematics.

Questions created philosophy.

Questions created science.

Questions created artificial intelligence.

Questions created civilizations.

Project BRAHMA believes that unanswered questions are among humanity's most valuable intellectual resources.

Therefore,

this book exists to preserve them.

---

# Purpose

The purpose of this document is **not to provide answers.**

Its purpose is to preserve:

* unanswered questions
* partially answered questions
* forgotten questions
* impossible questions
* dangerous questions
* future questions

because every major scientific revolution began as an unanswered question.

---

# Philosophy

Project BRAHMA does not assume that every question has an answer.

It also does not assume that every answer is final.

Questions evolve.

Answers evolve.

Understanding evolves.

Therefore,

questions themselves deserve preservation.

---

# The Question Principle

A question should never be discarded merely because:

* it is difficult,
* it cannot currently be tested,
* it appears philosophical,
* it has remained unanswered for centuries.

Many philosophical questions eventually became scientific questions.

---

---

# Categories of Questions

## Category A — Solved Questions

Questions with strong scientific consensus.

Example:

Why do objects fall?

---

## Category B — Partially Solved Questions

Questions with multiple competing explanations.

Example:

How does memory work?

---

## Category C — Open Questions

Questions currently lacking scientific consensus.

Example:

What is consciousness?

---

## Category D — Foundational Questions

Questions that influence multiple scientific disciplines.

Example:

What is information?

---

## Category E — Grand Questions

Questions concerning the nature of reality itself.

Example:

Why does anything exist?

Questions are preserved independently of beliefs.

Project BRAHMA records questions even when:

Curiosity has priority over certainty.
